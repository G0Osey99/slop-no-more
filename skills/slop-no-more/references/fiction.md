# Fiction and narrative

Source: StoryScope (Russell, Rajendhran, Pham, Iyyer & Wieting, 2026; 10,272 prompts, each written by a human and by five LLMs: Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3 Flash, DeepSeek V3.2, Kimi K2.5; 304 narrative features). The figures below are human vs AI shares or means, recomputed from the released feature file (61,575 stories). Every row listed points the same direction for all five models.

Use the human figure as the target band, not the opposite extreme. Human chronological discontinuity averages 2.4 out of 5, not 5. Pick the 3–5 moves this story needs rather than applying every one.

## Diagnose in passes (one group at a time, quote evidence)

**1. Theme over-determined (AI high)**
- Narrator explains what events mean: 51% → 76%. Thematic explicitness and moralizing: 3.28 → 3.94 (1–5).
- Dialogue turns into philosophical debate: 34% → 59%.
- The ending comments on itself: 37% → 47%.
- Fix: cut the lines that state the lesson, let dialogue serve wants and conflict, and end on an image or action, not an interpretation.

**2. Body and senses performed (AI high)**
- Emotion rendered as embodied sensation (tight chest, cold sweat) rather than named or shown through behavior: 39% → 81%. Explicit emotion labels ("she was afraid"): 29% human vs 8% AI.
- Setting mirrors mood (3.6 → 4.1); smell everywhere (57% → 82%).
- Fix: name an emotion plainly once, show one through action, and keep at most one or two embodied peaks. Let the weather be just weather.

**3. Streamlined structure (AI high)**
- No subplots: 57% → 79% (StoryScope Table 16). One unbroken causal chain. The resolution comes from the protagonist's own choice (46% → 69%) or from internal understanding (27% → 47%).
- The character is introduced by external description (30% → 52%), and the opening is spatially over-grounded.
- Fix: add nothing invented in an edit. When revising, let an existing secondary thread run alongside the main one, let the resolution come partly from outside, and introduce characters mid-dialogue or mid-action.

**4. Time kept linear (human high)**
- Chronological discontinuity: 2.40 vs 2.12. Anachrony (flashback, flash-forward): 2.59 vs 2.31. Nonlinear framing that delays disclosure: 1.96 vs 1.68. Recontextualization after a surprise: 3.29 vs 2.95.
- Fix: open later in the timeline, or hold back one fact that changes how earlier scenes read.

**5. A world without outside references (human high)**
- Explicit named references to real texts or authors: 46% vs 24%. Pop-culture and brand names: 40% vs 13%. Direct address to the reader: 9% vs 2%.
- Fix: use real, correct names where the world has them. Never fabricate a title or quote.

**6. Style (the dimension most "humanizers" never touch)**
- Extended conceit present: 40% → 83%. Recurring metaphorical motif: 69% → 96%. Figurative density 3.0 → 3.7. Fresh figures are rarer in AI than conventional ones.
- Earnest or lyrical tone: 40% → 71%. Ironic or wry tone: 36% → 13%.
- Parallel or list-like sentence structures: 70% → 99%. Loose multi-clause chains: 76% → 55%. Mostly paratactic: 31% → 14%.
- Consistently elevated register: 11% → 40%. Mixed register with code-switching: 56% → 19%.
- Prominent alliteration: 79% → 98%. Marked prose rhythm: 3.17 → 3.61.
- Fix: drop the controlling metaphor, let one character be funny, let sentences sprawl with "and", mix registers in dialogue, and break the cadence.

## Fiction lexicon (scan with `--genre fiction`)
These words and phrases appear more than 5× their general-English frequency and more than 3× their rate in Brown-corpus fiction, in 4 or more of the 5 models: *the weight of, a long moment, hung in the air, something shifted, the architecture of, at the edges of, the silence of, the ghost of a smile, smelled of, a sound like, less like X and more like Y, on a Tuesday, flickered, hummed, thrum, stillness, unhurried, faintly, unreadable, gestured, paused, whispered, flinched, pulsed, murmur, gaze, lingered, unmistakably, impossibly, composure, tenderness, unease, hushed, methodical, weathered.* As with every list, one hit means nothing and clusters do.

## Model habits (StoryScope §5, as priors only)
Claude: flattest escalation, epilogues and flash-forward endings, quiet endings, uncanny settings. GPT: gossip as a plot device, a distant retrospective narrator. Gemini: bleak settings, extended denouements, frequent flashbacks. DeepSeek: front-loaded context. Never guess the source model from the prose.

## Names
Models reuse a small pool of names (Elara, Kael, Mara, Thorne, Silas; Emily and Sarah in AI articles). Keep the author's names. When writing new fiction, choose names from the story's place, generation, and class.
